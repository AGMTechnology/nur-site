How to Memorise a Surah Fast and Keep It

4 min readThe Nūr team

To learn a surah quickly, break it into short passages. Listen to each passage being recited, read it yourself, then recite it from memory. Most of the time you save comes later: revise a surah the next day and you won’t have to learn it all over again.

Should you listen to a surah before you learn it?

Yes, and more than once. Start by listening to the whole surah with the text in front of you. Your ear picks up the rhythm, the pauses and the sound of each word before you say any of them.

Choose one reciter and stick with him. You’ll hear the same melody every time, and that melody helps you remember the order of the words. Go for a slow recitation where every sound is clear, such as Shaykh Maḥmūd Khalīl al-Ḥuṣarī’s.

Can’t read Arabic yet? You can still learn a short surah by ear, by listening and repeating. Learning to read will help you later with longer surahs.

How many verses should you learn in one session?

Start with about three verses, or roughly fifty words. That’s the most common passage size in Nūr, which divides the whole Quran into 1,710 passages.

Count the words too, because verses vary a lot in length. The three verses of al-Kawthar come to ten words in total. Verse 282 of al-Baqarah has 128 words on its own.

Take each passage through these four steps:

  1. Listen to it two or three times, following the text.
  2. Read along with the reciter, word by word.
  3. Read it on your own, out loud, without the audio.
  4. Recite it from memory, then check it against the text.

Once a passage has stuck, add the next one. If it won’t stick, split it in two. The right size is whatever you can recite from memory by the end of the session.

Can you learn a surah in one day?

For a short surah, yes. Al-Ikhlāṣ has four verses and fifteen words, so one or two sessions may be enough. A long surah is another matter. Al-Kahf has 110 verses and takes several weeks.

The real test comes the next day. A surah you learn in an hour and then leave alone soon fades. Recite it again the next day, and again the week after, and it stays with you.

The Quran itself speaks of this ease. In Surah al-Qamar, Allah says: وَلَقَدۡ يَسَّرۡنَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ لِلذِّكۡرِ فَهَلۡ مِن مُّدَّكِرࣲ‏ (54:17). It means: “We have made the Quran easy to remember. Is there anyone who will remember?” The same sentence appears four times in that surah.

The real test comes the next day.

Learning a long surah like al-Kahf or Yā-Sīn

Learn a long surah as a series of short ones. Split it into passages that follow the meaning, for example where a story begins. Al-Kahf has 110 verses, so passages of three or four verses come to about thirty sessions. Yā-Sīn (Yaseen) has 83 shorter verses, so passages of five or six come to about fifteen.

Each day, start by reciting the passages from previous days, then add today’s. Always link the new passage to the one before it: recite the end of one and the start of the next without stopping. Memory most often gets stuck where two passages meet.

A long surah takes effort, and that effort counts. The Prophet ﷺ said that someone who recites the Quran with ease is with the noble and righteous angels. And someone who recites it with difficulty, stumbling over the words, gets two rewards (reported by al-Bukhārī, no. 4937, and Muslim, no. 798).

How to avoid forgetting a surah you learned quickly

Revise it at longer and longer intervals: the next day, three days later, a week later, then a month later. Each time, recite it from memory before you look at the text. The effort of remembering is what makes it stick. If a passage has slipped, revise it sooner next time.

The Prophet ﷺ told people to keep returning to the Quran, because it slips away faster than camels slip free of their hobbles. Al-Bukhārī (no. 5033) and Muslim (no. 791) both report it. It’s a practical image: a camel you keep an eye on stays put.

For the full method, and how long the whole Quran takes, see How to Memorise the Quran Without Forgetting It.

Do you need to understand the meaning of the verses?

Not to start with. But the meaning helps a great deal when it comes to remembering. When you know what a passage says, you know what comes next, so the rest comes back to you more easily.

Before you learn a passage, read what it means in your own language. A sentence or two is enough. You don’t need to speak Arabic for that.

Stories help too. Al-Kahf tells of young men who took refuge in a cave, where Allah made them sleep for years. Al-Fīl tells of an army that came with an elephant, and of how Allah stopped it. Once you know the story, you can follow the surah as it unfolds.
